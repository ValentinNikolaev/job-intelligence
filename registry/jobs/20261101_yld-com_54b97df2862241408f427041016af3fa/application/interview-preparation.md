## Recruiter / HR Screening

Prepare a concise explanation of why this six-month Go contract fits the work you want to do. Lead with owning backend services from design into production, then explain the appeal of joining a client team with a defined outcome. Do not imply an extension is guaranteed. Ask about the client, rate basis, expected start and whether the engagement is employment or independent contracting.

Location is the first screening question. The posting body says remote EU-based work and GMT alignment, but the live badge says Germany only. State Rome, Italy accurately and ask whether Italy is accepted. Confirm working-hour overlap rather than assuming CET residence settles it. Provide only current availability, notice period and daily-rate expectations. Older availability answers are not a present commitment. Use the evidenced upper-intermediate English description and do not invent an Italian level.

CRURATED was concurrent part-time consulting. Explain that the overlap with Simple.life was deliberate, rather than a second standard full-time job. Simple.life is completed work. If asked about later project dates, distinguish delivery evidence from employment chronology rather than improvising a new end date.

## Culture Fit / Behavioral Interview

Prepare six evidence-based stories, keeping the situation, your own decisions, other people's contributions and observable results separate.

1. Describe taking a sensitive system from design to release. Use telephony architecture, security decisions, formal review and production delivery. Explain what frontend and SRE colleagues owned.
2. Explain a boundary introduced to make change easier. Use the helpdesk gateway and vendor-neutral events. Discuss what the abstraction covered and what remained vendor-specific.
3. Describe improving a system already in production. Use pipeline consolidation and removal of repeated full-history reads. Keep internal code counts out of the headline answer.
4. Explain coordinating delivery with competing concerns. Use airSlate team assignments from planning through release. State actual responsibility rather than claiming unilateral product authority.
5. Describe compatibility during change. Use CRURATED's parallel event-version publication and distinguish shipped behaviour from unverified cost trade-offs.
6. Explain a technical decision to a non-specialist. Use privacy and access controls around phone data, focusing on who could see what and why.

Do not manufacture a conflict, failure or lesson for a STAR answer. If the supplied facts do not answer the question, choose another genuine example or explain the limits of the available story.

## Technical Interview

High Priority — Go service design. Review contexts, cancellation, interfaces, error handling, concurrency, bounded work and API contracts. Practise explaining the actual support and gateway boundaries. A coding exercise may test clarity and correctness rather than framework recall. Write tests for observable behaviour and discuss what unit, integration and end-to-end checks each establish. TDD is explicitly mentioned, so explain your real test-writing process without adopting a label you cannot defend.

High Priority — distributed delivery. Walk through duplicate events, retries, idempotency, failure isolation and tenant-specific state. Explain where delivery guarantees stop and how operational evidence reveals an incomplete operation. Distinguish a proposed design from one you shipped. Prepare a queue-backed webhook design with retry limits and an explicit failure path, labelled as an interview design rather than past experience.

High Priority — architecture and operations. Use telephony ownership to explain service responsibilities, access decisions and rollout review. Review the AWS identity and queue concepts actually used, plus Kubernetes and release tooling at defensible depth. Practical Docker experience is confirmed. Prepare the actual container build, debugging and deployment details you can defend. Do not revive the withdrawn airSlate migration as a deployment story. Be clear about which parts of a deployment you owned and where SRE colleagues participated.

Medium Priority — data and performance. Discuss MySQL/PostgreSQL access patterns, query diagnosis and the difference between throughput and latency. Use qualitative airSlate database stability unless a metric is separately defensible. Review schema evolution and the interaction between consumers and publishers. For a new system-design question, identify the consistency requirements before selecting a database or queue.

Medium Priority — AI-assisted delivery. Distinguish building production AI integrations from using AI coding assistants. The former is evidenced, while the latter needs your own accurate explanation. Discuss validation and engineering judgment without claiming an undocumented tool workflow.

Low Priority — client-specific domain knowledge. No client domain is published. Learn that context once disclosed instead of preparing an invented fintech or healthcare case.

## CV Deep-Dive Questions

Be ready to defend what you owned before implementation, which decisions needed review, who operated the result and how you knew it was ready. For telephony, explain the privacy boundary and formal sign-off without presenting legal expertise or compliance certification as your own credential. For the gateway, distinguish the first B2B demonstration from broad external adoption.

For CRURATED, explain why parallel event versions were needed and what production ownership meant in a part-time engagement. For airSlate, separate personal development from coordination of team output. Explain why the CV retains older dated roles without narrative rather than inventing unrelated achievements. No metric is necessary merely to make the answer sound senior.

## Company-Specific Preparation

YLD describes work inside client teams and retaining client capability after an engagement. Prepare to discuss how you make an interface understandable and a technical decision reviewable. Treat onboarding and handover as likely discussion topics, not published obligations. The advertised client-partner conversation is a reason to explain technical choices through their effect on delivery and service operation.

## Preparation Plan

Must prepare: resolve Italy eligibility, confirm contract terms and current availability, and rehearse the telephony and gateway stories. Before technical: practise a small Go service exercise, explain testing honestly and review queues, API boundaries and failure cases. Before final/culture: prepare how you would enter a client codebase, communicate a trade-off and leave decisions understandable. Bring actual examples rather than promises of instant transformation.
