## Culture Fit / Behavioral Interview

Prepare 5–10 STAR stories: (1) defining the Simple.life calling-platform architecture and working with frontend/SRE through review and release; (2) handling sensitive-data boundaries; (3) unifying three Snowflake-to-Intercom workflows; (4) owning CRURATED DataLake evolution; (5) improving airSlate API performance; (6) improving delivery flow through CI/CD; (7) keeping seasonal email delivery reliable at PDFfiller. For each, state situation, personal contribution, collaborators, measurable or factual consequence and what was learned. Do not claim sole credit for platform-wide results.

Likely questions: How do you make an ambiguous platform decision? How do you influence a team without authority? How do you balance reliability and delivery speed? How do you decide whether to consolidate services? How do you document a sensitive-data design? Why Cision? Why now? What feedback changed your approach? Anchor responses in the stated stories rather than generic leadership claims.

## Technical Interview

**High Priority:** API/service design, event ingestion and contracts, data transformation boundaries, SQL/data-model reasoning, reliability/observability, CI/CD, security-sensitive backend design, Kubernetes concepts and production incident trade-offs. These map directly to the posting and CV. **Medium Priority:** distributed-systems fundamentals, operational dashboards, cost/reliability discussions, schema versioning and access controls. **High Priority:** technical-user search/filter interfaces, because they are central to the full-stack role. Clarify the actual frontend framework and give an honest account of current hands-on experience. **Low Priority:** unrelated application frameworks not named by the team.

Be ready to whiteboard a pipeline that ingests semi-structured operational events, validates/version-controls them, persists/query-enables them, exposes an API and handles retries, observability and access controls. Explain what you would measure before and after release. Do not imply direct Docker experience unless a real example is available.

Use a STAR answer structure: Situation—one operational-data or sensitive-data problem; Task—your personal decision boundary; Action—design, coordination and delivery choices; Result—the sourced production consequence. For the calling-platform story, say that the candidate owned architecture and backend, worked with frontend/SRE, and delivered a security-reviewed production release; do not add a made-up incident. Study the role’s explicit UI/search/filtering needs as **High Priority**: prepare to explain how backend API and data-shape choices support technical-user workflows, then state honestly that direct React experience is not in the CV.

For system design, begin by clarifying event volume, retention, query latency, access model and failure tolerance. Propose ingestion validation, versioned contracts, durable processing, relational/query storage, role-limited APIs, metrics and runbooks. Explain a rollout plan with a narrow first slice, observability and feedback from infrastructure users. This demonstrates reasoning without asserting unsupported tools.

## Preparation Plan

**Must prepare:** location/eligibility statement; five STAR stories; data pipeline/system-design sketch; exact boundaries for each CV metric. **Pre-technical:** rehearse SQL, schema evolution, ingestion, observability, CI/CD and Kubernetes discussions; identify any real Docker example before mentioning it. **Pre-final/culture:** articulate technical influence, written communication and interest in internal platforms; prepare salary/availability answers once confirmed.

Study plan: first, rehearse the two-minute eligibility statement and five bounded stories. Finally, practise a peer-design-review explanation that separates an independent decision from team influence. Prepare a one-sentence answer for every listed unknown: UI experience, Docker and Italy employment eligibility.
