# Interview Preparation

## Recruiter / HR Screening

Resolve these points before technical work:

- Does “Germany-based with the right to work here” exclude an Italy-based candidate, even if remote within the EU?
- What proportion of weekly work is React/TypeScript versus Go?
- Is the company open to a backend-heavy engineer ramping up on the frontend stack?
- What is the salary/equity structure for someone outside Germany, if permitted?
- What are notice-period, start-date, and travel/offsite expectations?
- Why this product and startup stage: connect the answer to product ownership and building from ambiguity, not invented manufacturing passion.
- Confirm the latest-role chronology and overlapping recent roles.

## Culture Fit / Behavioral Interview

1. Tell us about taking a product from early stage to users — use Hyprr prototype-to-beta delivery.
2. Describe end-to-end ownership — use the Simple.life Go automation platform.
3. Give an example of working with product or customer-facing teams — use Simple.life operations/product collaboration.
4. How do you handle shifting priorities — use startup roadmap work with the Hyprr CTO.
5. Tell us about improving an existing stack — use airSlate database and Kubernetes work.
6. Describe a technical trade-off you owned — prepare a real Hyprr or event-pipeline decision.
7. How do you approach a technology where you are not yet deep — answer with a concrete learning process, without claiming React experience.
8. Tell us about shipping under time pressure — use the under-six-month beta or BFCM peak.

## Technical Interview

- **High priority — Go:** service design, interfaces, error handling, contexts, concurrency, testing, API design, and operational behavior.
- **High priority — full-stack boundaries:** API contracts, authentication, validation, state ownership, pagination, error mapping, and coordinating frontend/backend changes.
- **High priority — take-home quality:** clear scope, tests, README, deliberate trade-offs, simple architecture, and working behavior. Do not hide missing React depth behind excessive backend complexity.
- **Medium priority — React/TypeScript:** components, props/state, hooks, async data, forms, error/loading states, type modeling, and testing. This requires focused preparation.
- **Medium priority — product system design:** translating user workflows into domain models, permissions, auditability, and iterative delivery.
- **Medium priority — AI integration:** deterministic boundaries around model calls, validation, retries, observability, and safe fallbacks, grounded in support automation.
- **Medium priority — cloud delivery:** AWS, Kubernetes, CI/CD, monitoring, and rollback.
- **Low priority — manufacturing standards:** learn basic requirements traceability and change management, but do not claim domain experience.

## CV Deep-Dive Questions

- What parts of the Simple.life Go platform did you own directly?
- How did AI-assisted triage integrate with deterministic backend workflows?
- What did you personally build in the CRURATED event system?
- How did Hyprr reach closed beta in under six months?
- Which frontend technologies have you used, and how would you ramp up on React/TypeScript?
- How did airSlate measure database and deployment improvements?
- Explain the overlapping Simple.life and CRURATED dates.

## Company-Specific Preparation

Review the [official product site](https://www.bowatt.com/) and understand the problem of turning large customer-requirement documents into structured analysis and collaboration. Review the published manufacturing use cases, but treat the outcomes as company claims. Prepare a simple product model for requirements, versions, comments, approvals, traceability, and AI-generated suggestions with human review.

## Preparation Plan

**Must prepare:** location/work-authorization answer; recent frontend evidence; a React/TypeScript ramp-up plan; one startup ownership story; one AI-integration story.

**Before the take-home/technical round:** build or refresh a small typed React client backed by a Go API, add tests and error states, and practice explaining what was deliberately left out.

**Before final/culture rounds:** prepare questions about founders, runway, customer discovery, equity terms, product decision rights, and how the team handles fast-changing priorities.

## Questions to Ask

1. Is Italy-based remote work compatible with the Germany eligibility requirement?
2. What is the current frontend/backend workload split?
3. How much React/TypeScript depth is expected on day one?
4. Which customer workflow is the highest product priority now?
5. How do engineering, product, and customer success decide what to build?
6. What does the take-home assess, and how much time should candidates spend?
7. What are the current engineering team size and deployment stack?
8. How are equity percentage, vesting, and dilution structured?
