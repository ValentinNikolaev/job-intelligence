# Interview Preparation — Digis Senior Laravel Developer

## Recruiter / HR Screening

Prepare a concise introduction: a senior PHP/Laravel backend engineer with 15+ years in software development, more than five years across Laravel products, and recent SaaS integration, reliability, and technical-leadership experience. Connect the return to Laravel to proven depth and hands-on product ownership; do not imply that CRURATED used Laravel or that Simple.life was PHP-based.

Expect direct screening on Vue.js. Answer candidly: the verified CV does not establish one year of commercial Vue.js experience. Do not relabel adjacent exposure as Vue. Ask how much independent Vue delivery is expected from day one and whether Laravel strength can offset the gap.

Clarify that CRURATED was a concurrent part-time consulting engagement alongside Simple.life, not a second full-time job. Confirm B2/Upper-intermediate English live. Establish exact answers for current status, start date, notice period, employment or B2B preference, and compensation. Rome supports EU location, but EU-wide work authorization is unknown; state the actual permit or citizenship position.

## Culture Fit / Behavioral Interview

Use STAR structure, but treat these as story pointers rather than scripted or invented answers:

1. **Tell me about a difficult production problem.** Use airSlate: investigate database/API bottlenecks, explain diagnostics and collaboration, and defend the reported 65% peak database load and 30% average API-response improvement.
2. **Describe a reliability improvement you owned.** Use Simple.life workflows across Zendesk, Intercom, and internal services: fallback logic, retries, monitoring, and operational consequences.
3. **When did you influence a technical direction?** Use Hyprr: roadmap work with the CTO and decisions around microservices, serverless delivery, CI/CD, and reliability while remaining hands-on.
4. **How have you handled exceptional scale?** Use PDFfiller: Laravel transactional email service, roughly 50 million emails monthly, and BFCM periods above 10x traffic; explain personal decisions and team boundaries.
5. **Tell me about knowledge sharing.** Use airSlate planning and 20+ technical interviews, or leadership of five backend engineers at PDFfiller. Focus on mechanism and outcome.
6. **Describe a migration with operational risk.** Use the airSlate ECS-to-Kubernetes migration or Simple.life platform-workflow migration; explain rollout safeguards, observability, and rollback thinking.
7. **How do you balance leadership and implementation?** Use Hyprr's 10-person team and prototype-to-beta delivery, separating coding from coordination.
8. **How do you enter an unfamiliar domain?** Discuss learning workflows, failure modes, and stakeholder priorities. Do not claim PropTech experience.

## Technical Interview

Prioritize these topics:

1. **Laravel architecture:** service boundaries, dependency injection, queues, events, validation, authorization, Eloquent trade-offs, migrations, testing, caching, and upgrades. Prepare one production architecture with exact individual contribution.
2. **Diagnostics-domain design:** model versioned questions, conditional flows, repair categories, problem types, localized content, and client-specific configuration. Discuss audit trails, validation, backward compatibility, permissions, and avoiding tenant-specific forks.
3. **End-to-end defect isolation:** start with reproducibility and correlation IDs, then trace browser evidence, Laravel logs, API contracts, queries, queues, and integrations. Distinguish investigating Vue symptoms from commercial Vue expertise.
4. **Integration reliability:** idempotency keys, retry policy, exponential backoff, dead-letter handling, schema/version compatibility, timeouts, rate limits, reconciliation, and observability. Anchor discussion in Simple.life and CRURATED.
5. **Scale and performance:** query plans, indexes, N+1 detection, caching, asynchronous work, backpressure, capacity tests, SLOs, and measurement. Use metrics only when you can explain baselines.
6. **Mature SaaS evolution:** feature flags, contract tests, phased rollouts, migrations, telemetry, accessibility collaboration, and preservation of existing client workflows.

For system design, practice a configurable repair-diagnostics service. Cover API/domain boundaries, multi-tenant configuration, versioning, auditability, events, failure recovery, privacy, monitoring, and rollout. Do not assume the unnamed platform's architecture.

## CV Deep-Dive Questions

Prepare defensible answers to: When did you last use Laravel in production, and what proves five-plus years? What did the airSlate Laravel/Symfony logging package do? How were the 65% database-load and 30% API-response figures measured? What did you personally build at CRURATED, with which PHP framework, and how was it separated from Simple.life? How was 10x throughput measured? Which Hyprr decisions were yours? What did leading five PDFfiller engineers involve? How were 50 million monthly emails observed? Explain title variations across roles. Be precise and never expand backend results into frontend ownership.

## Company-Specific Preparation

Digis presents itself as a Fiverr-backed development agency whose engineers embed in client teams. The role supports an unnamed property-operations platform and Diagnostics team. Credible motivation: Laravel-backed operational workflows, reliability at scale, technical decisions, and knowledge sharing.

Refer only to “the property operations platform,” “the client project,” or “the Diagnostics team.” Do not guess the client or describe the work as Fiverr's marketplace. Learn repair reporting, diagnostics, categorization, contractor coordination, compliance, and resident communication as preparation—not prior experience.

## Preparation Plan

Two days before: rehearse a 90-second introduction; write exact Laravel dates; choose four STAR stories; refresh testing, queues, authorization, Eloquent performance, and API design. One day before: diagram the diagnostics service; practice tracing a UI-to-integration defect; review Vue fundamentals without claiming commercial experience. On the day: confirm authorization, availability, engagement model, and compensation; prepare metric baselines; test English delivery. Afterwards, record unresolved stack, frontend-share, client-boundary, and process details.

## Questions to Ask

1. What proportion of the first six months is Vue.js delivery versus Laravel/backend and integration work?
2. Is one year of commercial Vue.js an absolute gate, or can deep Laravel experience plus a structured ramp-up be considered?
3. Which outcomes would define success in the first 30, 60, and 90 days?
4. How are core platform behavior and client-specific configurations separated without creating long-lived forks?
5. What are the most difficult reliability or defect-investigation problems facing the Diagnostics team today?
6. What are the current Laravel and Vue versions, testing approach, deployment process, and observability stack?
7. How are technical decisions made between Digis engineers, the client team, product stakeholders, and the wider engineering organization?
8. What onboarding and domain support is available for the housing, repairs, and compliance workflows?
9. What are the employment or B2B options for a candidate based in Italy, and which work-authorization evidence is required?
