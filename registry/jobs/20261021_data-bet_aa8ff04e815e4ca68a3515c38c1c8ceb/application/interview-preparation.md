## Culture Fit / Behavioral Interview

Prepare concise STAR outlines, separating context, personal decision, team involvement and verified consequence. Use these likely questions as prompts rather than memorized answers:

- Tell us about a service boundary you chose and why. Use the vendor-neutral helpdesk gateway, its tenant ingestion and the business-demo consequence. Explain alternatives only if personally remembered.
- Describe a reliability decision under external-system pressure. Use sustained operation of the Go support platform and distinguish platform responsibility from every individual automation scenario.
- When did you simplify a system without disrupting users? Use consolidation of Snowflake-to-Intercom processing and the elimination of repeated historical reads without downtime.
- Explain a trade-off between delivery speed and security. Use the telephony backend's design decisions, formal review and protection of customer phone data.
- How did you make an integration easier for other teams? Use CRURATED versioned event schemas and parallel versions; describe the supported consistency benefit.
- Describe a performance problem you helped solve. Use airSlate database workload redistribution and improved high-traffic stability, preserving backend-lead rather than sole-author attribution.
- Tell us about disagreement with product or operations colleagues. Select a real incident from supported cross-functional work; the sources establish collaboration but do not supply a disagreement narrative.

Do not add unsupported figures to strengthen a story.

## Technical Interview

High Priority — Go production engineering. Review contexts, cancellation, error handling, interfaces, testing seams, goroutines, channels, race detection and bounded concurrency. Practice a small event consumer with shutdown and cancellation. Explain how a service avoids leaking work when an upstream request ends. Exact primary-language duration needs a factual answer; broad backend tenure does not replace it.

High Priority — asynchronous and distributed design. Design a broker-backed processing service with idempotent handlers, retry policy, dead-letter handling, delivery semantics, ordering and backpressure. The candidate has RabbitMQ and event-driven experience, plus CRURATED downstream routing and technical ownership of production analytics. Distinguish observed behavior from guarantees and explain how a consumer recovers after partial failure. Do not claim that SQS and RabbitMQ were used interchangeably in the same role.

High Priority — databases. Review PostgreSQL query plans, indexes, transactions, locks, batching and safe schema evolution. MongoDB is a gap: study document modeling, indexing, atomicity boundaries and when a relational model is preferable. Present study as preparation, not employment experience. Expect questions on large datasets because the posting names them; ask for the actual scale before proposing an architecture.

High Priority — observability and operation. Separate metrics, logs, traces and profiles. Prometheus and Grafana have supported use; prepare a real explanation of what was observed and how decisions followed. Learn how Jaeger, Pyroscope and OpenTelemetry fit together, while acknowledging the limits of direct experience. Practice diagnosing latency across service boundaries and identifying whether CPU, database time or queue pressure dominates.

Medium Priority — Kubernetes and architecture. Review readiness, resources, rollback and scheduled jobs. Explain coupling, failure isolation and maintainability through real service boundaries. Low Priority — betting algorithms: the posting does not explicitly require them.
