## Strict Hiring Manager Review

**Go and backend ownership — strong.** The candidate confirmed five years of hands-on production Go, without assigning the entire period to Simple.life. At Simple.life, he built a vendor-neutral Go gateway and owned backend architecture for an outbound telephony platform. These demonstrate service boundaries, external integrations and delivery. They do not establish experience in every concurrency primitive listed in the vacancy.

**Distributed data and reliability — strong with boundaries.** The concurrent CRURATED PHP subcontract included technical ownership of a production DataLake, event analytics and parallel event versions. Its routing supported downstream delivery and backpressure. The support platform supplies a separate Go troubleshooting example: an incomplete-scan metric and partial-success handling after external API degradation. These are transferable systems problems, though neither proves time-series forecasting experience.

**APIs and databases — partial.** REST APIs and PostgreSQL appear in the candidate sources. gRPC and WebSocket development are not established. Redis and time-series databases are also not established. The CV avoids listing them as experience. A future screening discussion should establish the actual depth of PostgreSQL and any undocumented work before making a stronger claim.

**Ethereum — material uncertainty.** The Hyprr source says the candidate led backend development and worked on Ethereum-based digital asset components. It does not identify the integration interfaces, node clients, smart-contract responsibilities or production scope. Geth, Erigon, Ethereum JSON-RPC, subscriptions, transaction monitoring, EVM and cryptographic implementation cannot be inferred from that description. The role's blockchain section is extensive, so this gap could materially affect fit. Ask the candidate for a concrete account of that work before offering a project example.

**Infrastructure and delivery — partial.** The source describes AWS and Kubernetes CronJobs for Simple.life and lists CI/CD experience. An earlier analysis incorrectly treated an airSlate ECS-to-Kubernetes migration as evidence. The candidate withdrew that attribution, and it is excluded from the CV and this assessment. Docker and Linux depth also need clarification.

**Forecasting and energy — gap.** The candidate has worked with an LLM-assisted support agent and event analytics, but the record does not establish solar forecasting, renewable-energy assets, electricity trading or production ML forecasting. These are distinct domains. Discuss the adjacent engineering work without claiming previous industry experience.

Its two Hyprr bullets describe supported backend and product scope without naming a node client, contract or chain operation. No invented performance figures or blockchain technologies were added. Older roles retain concise, source-backed delivery and security context.

There are still questions a screening conversation should answer: how much of the candidate's Go work involved goroutines and synchronization, what precisely he built in Hyprr's digital-asset components, and whether the role requires current hands-on node infrastructure. The job text names Lugano while its Djinni metadata says worldwide remote. That does not establish the contract type or whether working from Italy is possible. No salary recommendation is made from the legal location alone.
