# Interview Preparation

## Recruiter / HR Screening

Likely questions:

- Why are you interested in Boardwise and this Technical Lead role?
- Are you comfortable with a remote-first team connected to Germany?
- What is your current location and working setup?
- What is your notice period and availability?
- What salary range are you targeting?
- How comfortable are you working in English day to day?
- How do you explain the shift from Go/PHP-heavy roles into a Microsoft/F#/ASP.NET environment?
- How hands-on do you want to remain as a Technical Lead?

Preparation notes:

- Be candid that your strongest production evidence is Go, PHP, AWS, Kubernetes, automation, messaging, and reliability rather than ASP.NET Core, F#, React, Azure, or Microsoft 365 APIs.
- Position the move as a technical leadership role where backend architecture, delivery ownership, team coaching, and AI-first engineering are strong transferable strengths.
- Use Simple.life for AI-assisted automation, airSlate for planning and delivery leadership, Hyprr for direct team management, and PDFfiller for high-volume communication infrastructure.
- Prepare a concise remote-work answer grounded in your location in Italy and experience collaborating across Product, Support Ops, AI, and engineering stakeholders.

## Culture Fit / Behavioral Interview

Likely behavioral questions:

- Tell me about a time you coached an engineer through a difficult technical or delivery problem.
- Describe a time you had to balance technical debt against product delivery.
- Tell me about a production issue you helped resolve.
- Give an example of making a pragmatic architecture decision under uncertainty.
- Describe how you work with Product Managers when scope or priorities change.
- Tell me about a time you improved team delivery.
- How do you introduce engineering standards without slowing the team down?
- How have you used AI in real engineering work?
- Tell me about a time you had to align multiple stakeholders.
- Describe a hiring or interviewing experience that changed how you evaluate engineers.

Real CV experiences to turn into STAR stories:

- airSlate Technical Lead: planning with Product and Development, assigning work from draft to release, and improving feature delivery rate by approximately 20%.
- airSlate Senior Software Developer: ECS-to-Kubernetes migration with Helm, GitHub Actions, and ArgoCD, contributing to cost reduction and performance improvement.
- Simple.life: OpenAI-powered auto-triage flows automating or deflecting up to 30% of inbound tickets.
- Hyprr: leading 10 developers and bringing a product from prototype to closed beta in under 6 months.
- PDFfiller: leading 5 backend engineers and scaling transactional email infrastructure to around 50 million emails per month.

## Technical Interview

High Priority:

- Technical leadership and architecture tradeoffs: central to the role.
- Backend system design for SaaS: required by the vacancy and strongly supported by candidate evidence.
- Production reliability, observability, incident handling, and performance optimization: key role responsibility and strong candidate evidence.
- AI-assisted engineering workflows: Boardwise explicitly emphasizes daily AI usage and coaching.
- Planning, scoping, estimation, and delivery under changing requirements: explicit leadership requirement.

Medium Priority:

- Cloud-native architecture: candidate has AWS/Kubernetes evidence; Boardwise mentions cloud-native and Azure.
- Event-driven architecture: vacancy lists Event-Driven Architecture and nice-to-have patterns; candidate has event-driven analytics and messaging evidence.
- Security, compliance, and governance context: candidate has GDPR/PCI DSS exposure and Boardwise operates in sensitive board-management workflows.
- API integrations: candidate has Zendesk, Intercom, payment providers, and internal service integration evidence; Boardwise may ask about Microsoft 365 APIs.

Low Priority:

- Coding exercise in F#, C#, ASP.NET Core, React, or TypeScript: possible but less defensible from candidate evidence. Prepare honestly as a ramp-up area.
- React Native: only nice-to-have and unsupported.
- Deep Microsoft Graph API specifics: important to Boardwise, but not supported by candidate evidence.

Topics to prepare:

- How to design a secure document/workflow backend with auditability, access control, versioning, and integration boundaries.
- How to structure AI-assisted code review, testing, debugging, documentation, and architecture workflows without weakening engineering standards.
- How to approach a new stack ramp-up plan for F#, ASP.NET Core, React, Azure, and Microsoft 365 APIs.
- How to lead architecture decisions while remaining hands-on.

## CV Deep-Dive Questions

Likely questions and evidence to defend:

- "What exactly did you lead at airSlate?" Explain planning with Product and Development, release plans, work distribution, delivery metrics, and technical interviews.
- "How did the delivery rate improve by approximately 20%?" Describe the metrics and process improvements without inventing additional numbers.
- "What did you own at Simple.life?" Explain the Go-based support automation platform, Zendesk/Intercom integrations, auto-triage, message delivery pipelines, and cross-functional work.
- "How did the OpenAI triage flow work?" Explain the workflow at a high level without exposing proprietary details or claiming unsupported model internals.
- "What was your role in the Kubernetes migration?" Describe ECS-to-Kubernetes migration preparation, Helm charts, GitHub Actions, ArgoCD, cost reduction, and performance improvement.
- "How did you manage 10 developers at Hyprr?" Explain product roadmap, OKRs, architecture direction, coordination with CTO, and delivery to closed beta.
- "How did PDFfiller scale to 50 million emails per month?" Discuss infrastructure, technical decisions, deliverability concepts, and peak traffic operations.
- "Why is CRURATED not prominent in this CV?" Explain only if asked: the tailored CV prioritizes the most relevant leadership and AI/backend evidence while preserving factual chronology.

## Preparation Plan

Must-prepare before recruiter screen:

- A 60-second explanation of why Boardwise is relevant despite the Microsoft stack gap.
- A transparent stack-gap answer with a concrete learning plan.
- Current availability, salary range, remote-work expectations, and English working comfort.
- Three leadership stories: airSlate delivery metrics, Hyprr team leadership, PDFfiller team scaling.

Pre-technical interview:

- Review F#, C#, ASP.NET Core, React, TypeScript, Azure, Microsoft Graph, and Microsoft 365 API basics enough to ask informed questions and explain ramp-up.
- Prepare one system design example for a secure board-document workflow with audit trails and integrations.
- Prepare one AI-assisted engineering workflow example from Simple.life.
- Prepare one reliability/performance example from airSlate.

Pre-final / culture interview:

- Prepare examples for ownership, pragmatism, fast decision-making, coaching, cross-team alignment, and balancing quality with speed.
- Prepare a concise answer about leading by example while staying hands-on.
- Prepare questions about engineering culture, AI adoption standards, product maturity, and expectations for the first 90 days.

## Questions to Ask

- What are the first technical and team outcomes expected from this Technical Lead in the first 90 days?
- How is the engineering team organized today, and how many engineers would this role directly coach or lead?
- How much of the role is hands-on coding versus planning, architecture, reviews, and people development?
- Which parts of the Microsoft 365 integration are most critical to the product roadmap?
- How mature is the current F#, ASP.NET Core, React, and Azure stack?
- How does Boardwise define "AI-first engineering" in day-to-day delivery and code review?
- What engineering standards are already established for testing, observability, security, and architecture decisions?
- Where does the team currently feel the most delivery friction?
- How do Technical Leads coordinate across teams and Product Managers?
- What would make a new Technical Lead successful in Boardwise's remote-first environment?
