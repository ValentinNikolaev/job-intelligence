## Strict Hiring Manager Review

### Strengths

1. **Direct PHP/MySQL production work.** Recent product experience covers backend development, database load, queries, and API performance.
2. **Integration and process automation.** Simple.life provides current evidence of external systems connected through a maintained backend.
3. **Operational ownership.** The candidate has repeatedly carried work through deployment, monitoring, incident diagnosis, and improvement.

### Material weaknesses

1. **Framework-free PHP is not explicit.** This matters because the role deliberately names PHP without frameworks. Safe rewrite: emphasize core PHP experience without asserting a recent framework-free codebase.
2. **Frontend depth is limited.** The vacancy asks only for basic tasks, but jQuery is still named. Safe rewrite: “practical JavaScript, HTML/CSS, and Bootstrap exposure” rather than “full-stack expert.”
3. **Seniority may exceed role scope.** A 15+ year profile and lead experience could raise retention or compensation questions. Safe rewrite: foreground hands-on delivery and avoid title inflation.

Two review passes retained the strongest database and integration evidence, then removed wording that overstated frontend breadth or implied logistics-domain experience.

## Red Flags

- The candidate's strongest recent PHP work used Laravel/Symfony. Prepare a concrete explanation of how core PHP knowledge transfers to a no-framework codebase.
- JavaScript/HTML/CSS/Bootstrap evidence exists but is less recent; jQuery depth is unknown.
- The candidate has e-commerce in the broader career record, but recent roles do not prove logistics-product experience.
- Source records conflict on the latest-role end date. Confirm chronology before submission.
- The vacancy's middle/senior range may require early alignment on scope, autonomy, and compensation for a highly experienced applicant.
