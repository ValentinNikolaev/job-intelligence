# **Position Summary**

Every new laboratory that implements our AI for routine diagnostics is a project with a lot of moving parts. The software is connected to the laboratory's image management and information systems, our AI is validated on their cases, pathologists are trained, and the requirements of their market and accreditors are met. Each of these projects brings together laboratory directors, pathologists and IT teams on the customer side, scanner and software partners, and our product, ML, engineering and quality teams internally.

As Clinical Implementation Manager you lead those projects. You own the plan, direct the specialists who do the technical work, and carry the go-live date. Once a laboratory is live you stay with them as their main contact, owning support and our long-term success with the partner. This is not a sales role and you do not carry a sales target.

# What you'll own

## **Onboarding projects**

-

Build and own the project plan for each new laboratory, from kickoff to go-live, with dependencies and dates kept current.

-

Direct the work across our product, ML, engineering and quality teams and the customer's staff.

-

Accompany the validations customers run as part of onboarding. The laboratory's medical director designs the study; you make sure the technical setup is correctly installed and working, that our teams deliver what the validation depends on, and that the customer has everything they need to complete it.

-

Carry the committed go-live date, and raise risks early with options rather than after a slip.

## **Customer success after go-live**

-

Act as the customer's main point of contact once live, monitoring usage and engagement and reaching out proactively.

-

Own incoming support requests: resolve what you can, coordinate product and engineering on the rest, and close the loop with the customer.

-

Bring what you learn from customers into product planning, and flag potential expansion opportunities to Sales where relevant.Bring what you learn from customers into product planning.

You do not carry a sales target. But flagging expansion opportunities to Sales is part of your responsibility.
