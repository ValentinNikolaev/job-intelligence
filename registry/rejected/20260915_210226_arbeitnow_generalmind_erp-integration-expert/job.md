## Tasks

- Own the integration architecture for new customers end to end: assess their ERP landscape, choose the right interface pattern (OData, RFC/BAPI, IDoc/EDI, CPI/BTP, staging tables, file-based), and design something their IT will approve and operate.

- Lead technical discovery with customer IT, basis, and process teams, and surface the blocking constraint in week one, not week nine.

- Own the data and field mapping from the customer's order, item, partner, and pricing structures onto our internal model, including the custom fields that carry the real business logic.

- Design and build our ERP connectors in Python (S/4HANA and ECC, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, Salesforce) and turn per-customer work into reusable abstractions, so the tenth connector costs a fraction of the first.

- Build durable, long-running integration workflows with safe write-back into customer systems of record: idempotency, retries, replay semantics, and recovery paths from the start.

- Own integration observability: know when a sync is stuck, degraded, or silently writing the wrong thing before the customer notices.

- Define the authentication, network, and security posture per customer and get it through their review.

- Feed what you learn in customer landscapes back into the product. You will see the same five problems before anyone else does.

## Requirements

- ERP integration depth, non-negotiable: you have delivered productive ERP integrations and know the interface landscape first-hand (IDoc, BAPI/RFC, OData/Gateway, CPI/BTP Integration Suite), on both S/4HANA and ECC, and you know which one to reach for and why.

- Functional grounding: fluent in at least one of SD (order-to-cash) or MM (procure-to-pay), credible in the other, plus the FI touchpoints for AP/AR. You know what a schedule line, a condition record, and a three-way match actually are.

- Engineering, not just configuration: 5+ years building and operating backend systems with real Python depth, owning production services rather than just delivering features.

- Reliability instinct: idempotency, retries, and observability are part of how you design, not afterthoughts. A workflow engine like Temporal in production is a strong plus.

- Customer-facing credibility: you can stand in front of an enterprise IT department, absorb their constraints, push back where it matters, and leave them confident.

- End-to-end ownership: you identify what's broken or missing before you're asked, and you don't leave the system worse than you found it.

- Professional English; German is a strong advantage given our customer base.

- You're happy being in the office five days a week.
