### About the Role

A fast-growing, seed-stage enterprise AI platform is looking for a talented and proactive **Full-Stack Product Engineer** to join the team on-site in Berlin. Founded in 2023, the company builds a comprehensive, security-first platform that helps organizations securely adopt and integrate generative AI — covering AI chat, workflows, custom agents, and integrations across entire business operations.

This is an early-stage opportunity where you'll own features end-to-end, work directly with the Chief Product Officer, and ship AI-powered capabilities that drive enterprise adoption at scale.

### What You'll Do

-

Contribute to the development of product features across the AI platform, from technical design through to user-facing implementation.

-

Build end-to-end solutions spanning the frontend (Next.js), backend (Node.js), and database layers.

-

Collaborate with the design team and customers to gather insights, iterate on features, and refine user flows.

-

Implement user interfaces including full screens, reusable components, and complete user journeys.

-

Maintain and optimize the codebase for performance and user experience.

-

Participate in **Support Engineer rotations**, periodically shifting focus to assist customers and the customer support team alongside the rest of the engineering team.

### What We're Looking For

**Must-have:**

-

2+ years of experience in software engineering.

-

Strong proficiency with **Next.js, TypeScript, and Node.js** (dealbreakers).

-

Hands-on experience with **PostgreSQL, Redis, Prisma, tRPC, and Tailwind CSS**.

-

Proven track record of delivering features end-to-end — from concept to production — in full-stack development.

**Nice to have:**

-

Experience building AI-powered product features and integrating AI capabilities into enterprise applications.

-

Experience collaborating closely with a Chief Product Officer and cross-functional teams to shape product direction.

-

Strong CS fundamentals with a habit of rapid learning and adaptation.
